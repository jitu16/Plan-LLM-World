#+TITLE: Geography and Territory
#+STARTUP: overview

* AGREED
- Genesis settlement has a protected radius and a hostile outside.
- Environmental mana quantity and replenishment vary geographically.
- The initial region graph is one Genesis zone with six neighboring unexplored zones. Unexplored does not mean an infinite reservoir: each outer zone has finite mana that changes with exchange.
- The world has spherical topology without map edges, using an icosahedron-derived dual grid of hexagonal and pentagonal zones.
- Exactly twelve pentagonal zones have five neighbors; remaining zones have six. Genesis begins in an ordinary six-neighbor zone.

* AGREED: Spherical geodesic grid
- Use an icosahedron-derived dual (Goldberg/geodesic) grid of hexagonal and pentagonal cells on a sphere.
- A conventional hexagonal zone has six neighbors; exactly twelve pentagonal zones with five neighbors are needed if the sphere contains only pentagons and hexagons meeting three at each vertex.
- Genesis can begin at a normal six-neighbor hexagonal cell, so the initial seven-zone model survives this transition.
- Store explicit adjacency lists. Diffusion, trade, travel, and monster movement use neighbors rather than assumptions about a rectangular map.
- The spherical surface has no edges; there is no need to connect opposite map edges as though they were a torus.
- For exchange and demographic calculations, use each cell's actual area and shared boundary geometry rather than assuming every zone has identical size.
- Allow world generation to be lazy: remote cells have aggregate environmental attributes while detailed terrain and NPC encounters are instantiated on exploration.

* REJECTED ALTERNATIVE: Hexagonal torus
- A pure hexagonal grid can use periodic identifications, but the resulting topology is toroidal rather than spherical.
- Rejected in favor of a spherical world, which has no map edges.
